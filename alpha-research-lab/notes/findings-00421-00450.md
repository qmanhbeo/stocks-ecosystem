## F440: H582 Co-fire Degree as Hidden Demand Signal — PASSED (2026-05-17, Session 379)

**Decision: PASSED (new regime signal)**

This experiment tested whether ticker-level cofire degree (how many other tickers share signal on same day) predicts signal quality. Hypothesis: low cofire (isolated, fewer competitors) = stronger signal.

**Results:**

| State | IC | Rows | Positive Years |
|-------|-----|------|----------------|
| all_rows baseline | 0.1277 | 821,764 | 11/11 |
| signal (C23) | 0.1564 | 68,133 | 11/11 |
| signal + isolated (Q1) | 0.2064 | 10,427 | 11/11 |
| signal + crowded (Q4) | 0.1259 | 21,475 | 9/9 |

**Lifts:**
- Isolated vs signal: +0.0500
- Crowded vs signal: -0.0305
- Isolated vs crowded: +0.0805

**Key insight:**
1. **Cofire isolation is a significant signal conditioner**: Isolated signals (Q1) achieve IC=0.2064, +0.0500 lift over raw signal.
2. **Crowded signals are weaker than baseline**: Q4 cofire produces IC=0.1259, below the 0.1277 baseline.
3. **Mechanism**: This captures competitive crowding at the signal-detection level. When many stocks share the same signal (high cofire), the alpha is diluted by competition. When your signal is unique that day (low cofire), there's less competition to exploit the hidden demand.
4. **New regime signal**: "Cofire isolation" is orthogonal to existing signals - it's a meta-conditioner that measures signal crowdedness, not a signal itself.

**Verdict:** PASSED - Cofire degree is a strong, robust conditioner that should be added to the signal library.
