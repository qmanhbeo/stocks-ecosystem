# 100 Sessions In: The Survivors, The Graveyard, And The Rules We Earned


Written at Session 104, after the lab crossed the first hundred-session mark.

This is a celebration note, but not a victory lap.
The honest thing to celebrate is that the lab kept getting stricter.
More branches died than survived.
Several attractive backtests were killed.
Multiple narrow wins were demoted to offline evidence.
The live branch was rolled back when pseudo-live evidence said it should be.

That is the real achievement.

---

## What Actually Survived

### 1. The live branch survived by becoming simpler, not more clever

The current live branch is still:

- `full_library + k5_r5_eq + h175__rel2_gt0 + sticky_bonus_0p015`

The lab learned the hard way that the prettier historical winners were not automatically the more honest ones.
Blocked-buy vetoes, sector caps, and other defensive add-ons could improve the full-history chart and still fail the `2023-2025` pseudo-live ladder.
When that happened, the branch was rolled back.

That rollback was one of the most important decisions the lab made.

### 2. The signal library survived better than many portfolio branches

The strongest durable output of the lab is still the signal library, not a single magic trading rule.

The clearest survivor remains:

- `S1`

Around it, the lab built a family of candidate signals that are useful because they are predictive and different, not because each one is a standalone system.

By Session 104, even the reduced-shape intraday branch found its honest surviving form:

- not a live gate
- not a selector-state router
- but observed-subset disagreement atoms that remain orthogonal to `S1`, `C4`, and `C12`

### 3. Offline evidence became a first-class result, not a consolation prize

One of the best changes in the lab over the first hundred sessions was learning to classify results properly.

Some branches were real without being deployable.

Examples:

- breakout-trust and breakout-survivor local passes
- family-local reintegration passes
- reduced-shape intraday disagreement atoms

These matter because they narrow the map.
They tell us where the signal is, even when they do not produce a live rule.

## The Graveyard

Many things died honestly.
That is part of why the survivors are credible.

### 1. Pretty backtests died under harder protocols

Several branches looked strong until they were forced through:

- pseudo-live ladders
- current-account compatibility
- operational book-shape diagnostics
- broader-family reintegration

When they broke there, they were not rescued with more tuning.

### 2. Breakout-local timing kept finding signal and then failing transport

This was one of the defining patterns of Sessions 90-104.

The branch kept rediscovering a real local effect:

- predictive passes
- deterministic sleeve passes
- narrow reintegration passes

But then the same question kept killing it:

- does it survive in the actual book?

Often the answer was no.
The book became too narrow.
Holdings collapsed.
Cash rose.
Operational compatibility failed.

That repeated failure was not wasted work.
It taught the lab that "local edge" and "transportable edge" are different objects.

### 3. The top-k intraday stress router died for a good reason

This branch mattered because it tested a plausible fallback:

- maybe the reduced-shape intraday information belonged at the selector-state layer rather than the row-level gate

The lab tested it on the frozen sticky branch.
Coverage was strong.
The result still failed.

That was useful because it ruled out another tempting form of over-interpretation.

## The Rules We Earned

These are the real celebration items.
They were expensive to learn.

### 1. Pseudo-live beats narrative

If a branch wins on a broad historical slice and loses on the expanding-train / next-year ladder, the ladder wins.

No exceptions.

### 2. Operational compatibility matters more than local edge

A rule is not honest just because it improves a narrow sleeve.
If it breaks:

- holdings
- cash weight
- sparse-book rate
- replacement pressure

then the branch is not live-ready.

### 3. Offline evidence is real evidence

The lab stopped forcing every result into a false binary:

- live winner
- useless failure

That was a major maturity step.

### 4. New data, new label, or new contract

If a family is exhausted on the current stack, the lab should stop.
No more local tuning just to keep motion going.

This rule probably saved dozens of bad sessions.

### 5. The live branch stays frozen unless the burden is clearly met

This is the discipline that now protects the lab from its own creativity.

## What The Lab Knows Now

After one hundred sessions, the lab knows at least this much:

- the core long-only selector problem is real and tractable
- the strongest live branch is not the fanciest historical branch
- the signal library is a better durable asset than most local overlays
- intraday information can be real without being deployable as a gate
- branch closure is as valuable as branch promotion
- forward honesty matters more than backtest aesthetics

That is real progress.

The next hundred sessions should refuse:

- to confuse narrow predictive wins with deployable rules
- to rewrite the live branch without pseudo-live proof
- to touch `2026+` as a backdoor optimization surface
- to treat branch kills as failure instead of progress

## The Honest Celebration

The lab crossed one hundred sessions without giving up its standards.

It kept the workspace long-only.
It rolled back branches that looked great but failed harder tests.
It learned to separate:

- live promotion
- offline evidence
- branch kill

It kept a permanent record of what broke.
It got less impressed by beautiful charts.
It became harder to fool.

That is worth celebrating.
